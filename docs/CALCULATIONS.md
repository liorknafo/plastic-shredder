## 2. Cutting force and the torque the plastic needs

Model: a tooth shears a strip as wide as the blade out of a solid wall of thickness h, F = τ·t·h. Both shafts take part in each shear (the strip is cut between a blade of A and its neighbour on B), so the input torque is 2·k·F·r with r = 30 mm (middle of the overlap) and k = 1.3 for dull edges, crushing and friction. The 15° tooth helix spreads the 24 teeth of a shaft so that about one per shaft bites at a time. This is a conservative full-section shear model; 3D-print waste is mostly 0.8-3 mm walls.

### 3b. Hard jam: the torque spike from the parts downstream of the limiter

If something hard (a screw, a nut) stops the blades instantly, the limiter slips, but the parts after it (blades, spacers, shafts, gears, sprocket, chain, the limiter's own sprocket) must still be stopped by the jam. With a rigid jam and only the shaft's torsion as a spring (both pessimistic), the extra torque is ΔT = ω·√(J·k):

|---|---|---|

| Torsional stiffness k | 59,101 N·m/rad | hex AF 25.05 over 62 mm: the shortest path from a torque input (B's gear) to a blade |
| Shaft speed ω | 2.97 rad/s | 28.4 rpm |
| Spike ΔT | **43 N·m** |  |
| Peak = T_LIMIT + ΔT | **107 N·m** | checked below with SF ≥ 1.2 on yield and bearing s0 ≥ 1.0 |

Without the limiter on the output side, the worm and the gearbox pulley would add 50-150 N·m more (their inertia is multiplied by the gear ratio squared), which is why the limiter sits after the gearbox and not at the belt.

## 5. Blades

The bore is finished to the real shaft (25.1 AF on 25.05: 0.025 mm clearance per flank), so a blade turns 0.25° before the flank ends touch. One blade is assumed to take the whole torque (a single tooth jammed); friction from the stack clamp is ignored.

- **Elastic estimate (information only):** with a Winkler contact stiffness k ≈ (E/2)/(2l) = 9299 MPa/mm, 45 N·m turns the blade 0.43°; contact spreads from s = 3.3 to 5.6 mm and the edge pressure is ~165 MPa, above the mild steel's 235 MPa. So the bore edges **will bed in** by a few hundredths of a millimetre on first loading; the contact then spreads and later loads stay elastic (shakedown). Every sharp hex bore does this; the stiffness estimate is crude.
- **Capacity:** the bore can only collapse when every half-flank is at yield, T = 3·Sy·t·l²; that must exceed the jam limit by the static margin (first row below).
